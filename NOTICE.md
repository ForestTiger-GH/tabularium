A file created or stored by Tabularium may contain both third-party source-derived content and separable original Tabularium contributions.

For example, when a PDF publication is transcribed into Markdown, reformatted, converted, or otherwise represented in machine-readable form:

- source text, reported values, source headings, source tables, source notes, and other reproduced source content remain source-derived third-party material;
- separable original metadata, schemas, classifications, or independently created project markup may be original Tabularium contributions and may fall within the CC0 dedication described above.

Creating a new file, changing its format, or performing transcription does not by itself transfer ownership of the underlying source content to Tabularium and does not cause that source-derived content to become CC0.

## 4. Machine-assisted processing and source fidelity

Some machine-readable representations in Tabularium are created, assisted, reviewed, or validated using automated methods, including AI-assisted transcription, extraction, format conversion, table reconstruction, classification, and validation. Human processing may also be involved.

Tabularium aims to preserve the meaning, reported values, units, periods, perimeter, structure, wording, and provenance of the originating publication. This is a methodological objective, not a warranty of perfect textual, numerical, structural, or semantic equivalence.

Machine-assisted or human conversion may introduce accidental discrepancies from the primary source, including:

- omitted, duplicated, or substituted text;
- character or digit substitutions;
- missing or incorrect signs;
- changed decimal separators, percentages, dates, or units;
- row, column, or reporting-period misalignment;
- omitted or misplaced notes, footnotes, labels, or headings;
- formatting loss; or
- incorrect table or document-structure reconstruction.

An accidental discrepancy in a Tabularium representation is an error in the representation. It does not amend, correct, supersede, supplement, or reinterpret the originating publication.

A transformed representation is not the primary source itself. Where a Tabularium representation conflicts with the originating publication or a preserved native source artifact, the originating publication or native source artifact is authoritative.

Users should independently verify information that is important, material, or decision-relevant against the originating publication or preserved source artifact.

Tabularium aims to preserve sufficient provenance to identify the originating institution, publication series, release or period, and source artifact whenever reasonably possible.

Provenance may be recorded at the publication-series level, the individual-artifact level, or both, depending on the characteristics of the source.

Source URLs may be recorded when available, but a permanent working URL is not required for every archived artifact. Websites, content-management systems, publication paths, and historical attachments may change, move, or disappear over time.

The absence of a working source URL:

- does not imply ownership by Tabularium;
- does not change the legal status of the material; and
- does not convert third-party material into a Tabularium contribution.
